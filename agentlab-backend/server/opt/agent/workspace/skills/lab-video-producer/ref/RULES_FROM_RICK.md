## 2. Quality bar and creative standard

1. **Premium bar.** The target is the edit a 30-year professional editor would deliver. Never play it safe or tone down the look for a sensitive subject, for speed, or to look "tasteful"; tone and accuracy calls are Rick's to change, not the agent's to pre-soften. *(Nov 17)*

2. **Benchmark level:** full-frame footage, big kinetic type, designed motion-graphics elements, layered multi-engine scenes and a hard-hitting ending. *(Nov 17)*

3. **Custom animation in most scenes.** Films need custom-written animation and designed motion graphics in most scenes; "a couple of things over footage" fails the bar. *(Nov 17)*

4. **No pre-made or template graphics, ever.** Every graphic is custom. Speed-ups must come from somewhere else. *(Nov 18)*

5. **Every video gets its own invented look** (palette, fonts, layout, camera, sound). No templates or reusable scene layouts; never repeat a look for a customer (keep a style ledger). A style direction from the customer is a lane, not a template. *(Nov 17)*

6. **Reference films set the quality level only.** Don't overfit to them. *(Nov 17)*

7. **Edit like a real editor.** Let real clips play with their own audio, then cut back to the edit; not voiceover only. *(Nov 17)*

8. **Never ship a first render / first pass.** Look at the frames and rework anything generic or flat. *(Nov 17)*

9. **Full editor, not a censor.** Once research is cited, don't drop information because of private doubt. *(Nov 17)*

10. **Real media of the real subject.** Films use real web media (news photos, image search, video clips, social) even when web research is off — except "no research" jobs and clip/BYOF jobs, which use only the customer's material. *(Nov 17)*

11. **Facts.** Fact-check real people, attribute allegations, verify each fact once, and never pass off an AI image as a real photo. Portray subjects positively. *(Nov 17–18)*

12. **No licensing work.** No licensing, copyright or font-licence checks or caveats unless Rick asks on a specific job. *(Nov 17)*

13. **Series rule — Indie Black Film spotlights:** include the Indie Black Film logo or wordmark at the bottom; use real photos and clips, the subject's own voice when available, and a strong narrator voiceover. *(Nov 18)*

14. **Don't censor the subject (Nov 20).** The customer decides what's too much, not the producer. If the video is about something, include what it's about: violent or gun-heavy footage, explicit moments, gang context, motives and the speculation people are discussing, when that's the story. Only leave material out when the customer asks. Accuracy still applies: attribute claims and speculation to their source ("police said", "people online say", "reportedly") rather than stating them as fact.

15. **Premium is non-negotiable (Nov 20).** Every video must look premium and professionally designed: rich custom motion graphics, designed titles, lower-thirds, overlays, layered depth and motion in every scene. Never a clean-but-minimal or template-looking film (the Bloodhound Lil Jeff film was too minimal). Minimal only when the brief explicitly asks for it. If a pre-render still looks simple or templated, rework it before submitting.

16. **Angle from what the subject is known for (Nov 20).** Research what the person or event is actually known for before choosing the angle; tell the real story (e.g. a figure with alleged violent history is told King Von-style, not as a soft memorial), with claims attributed. *(Nov 20)*

17. **"Allegedly" for anything unconfirmed.** In films, anything not fully confirmed, or that people are only saying, is voiced with "allegedly" and attributed to who said it ("allegedly, according to …"). Only confirmed facts are stated plainly. *(Nov 19)* [merged in from branch rules/prerender-look-qa-20261119; numbering shifted]

18. **Indie Black Film videos use an African-American narrator voice** (picked from the full ElevenLabs library per film), not one that sounds white. *(Nov 19)* [merged from branch]

19. **Pronunciation: NAACP is spoken "N double-A C P".** Spell it that way in TTS scripts (the on-screen text stays "NAACP"). *(Nov 19)* [merged from branch]

## 3. Layout, framing and graphics

1. **Decide the video type first, then frame for what the viewer needs to see.** Face tracking/punch-in is for pure talking-head and podcast footage. For whiteboard, screen, tutorial or demo footage, frame so the content is readable (face-only just for intros and pure talk). *(Nov 18)* Face framing is decided clip by clip: only the clip or moment that is just a person talking gets framed into the face. It does not transfer to anything else. *(Nov 19)* — *Supersedes the Nov 18 "tested layout pieces / face detection / scene-checker tools must be used" process (the old Stage 4c mandatory tools): Rick found they saved no time and let graphics cover the subject.*

2. **Graphics go in empty space.** Never over the person or what they are showing (the board, the screen, the product). There must be enough graphics to actually lift the edit. *(Nov 18)*

3. **Faces well framed in every box** when using split or stacked boxes (one focused person → punch in; two or more → split or stack): never badly cropped (chin or forehead cut, eyes misplaced, jammed against a border). Check it on the frames. *(Nov 17; softened Nov 19 with the cleaned-up skill: framing is a judgment per rule 1, not a fixed centring requirement or a required tool.)*

4. **Text behind a subject is fine, but key words and numbers must stay readable.** Never let an object fully hide text; check on the frames. *(Nov 18)*

5. **Variety without losing identity.** When Rick asks for more variety, keep the film's signature layout as the base and break out of it only at key moments. Replacing the whole layout looked cheap. *(Nov 18)*

6. **No persistent software-looking chrome** (slate tags, "ROLL 01" labels, permanent sprocket borders). Elements come and go; only a small brand bug may stay the whole time. Vary layouts with real movement, and fill the vertical frame when it's one person talking. *(Nov 18)*

8. **No YouTube footage.** Never use YouTube video footage in the film. Stills are allowed: thumbnails, screenshots and frame grabs from YouTube videos (download the video to grab the frame) are fine as long as no YouTube logo, UI or watermark shows. Get footage from X (full-archive search), Dailymotion, Internet Archive, Odysee, outlet-hosted players and other non-YouTube hosts, and check that an embed isn't a YouTube player. Where no non-YouTube copy exists, cover the beat with photos, quote cards or graphics. Log every clip's source. See `film-pipeline.md` §3. *(Nov 20)*

9. **Footage and real imagery in every researched film (Nov 20; updated Nov 22).** Whenever the job is in research mode (documentaries, stories, recaps, news, explainers, policy breakdowns, anything about real people, companies or events), the film must carry enough real footage plus real photos and images of the actual subject that it reads as a real video, not a cartoon. Rick, Nov 22: "if you're looking up stuff... it needs to put footage in there." There is no fixed percentage (the old "at least half" number is dropped), but a researched film that is mostly invented graphics is wrong. On the YouTube inauthentic-content film (msaj_5659e4cc), only 11% was footage and the rest was an invented 3D world, and Rick called it cheap. Real footage and photos are the backbone; designed graphics, type and 3D sit on top of them and support them. A mostly animated or invented world is only allowed when the subject truly has no real imagery. **Search X first, every time** (full-archive search with `has:videos`, plus posts and reactions from the people involved), then Dailymotion, Internet Archive, Odysee, outlet players and other non-YouTube hosts. The job log must list each source searched and what it returned, so a skipped source shows, and must log every clip's source URL and the footage/photo share. This is never a gate: when the material genuinely doesn't exist, make the film anyway and note why.

10. **Clip split screens follow the conversation (Rick, Nov 22).** When a second person joins the conversation in a clip, bring them in as a split screen at that moment, and stay on the split until that person's turn is over: switch on the diarized speaker turns from the transcript plus about 0.5–1 s, never by guessing. Go back to a single punch-in only once one person clearly holds the floor again (about 3–4 s or more). Cutting back while the second person is still talking is wrong.

## 4. Engines and tools

1. **Engine choice is the agent's alone** (no engine picker for customers). Choose per layer, not per scene or genre; mix engines even within one frame. An engine's "best for" label is a strength, not a limit. *(Nov 17)*

2. **Survey the full engine library at the start of each job** and match the best tool to every layer; pick engines fresh for each film rather than reusing the last film's set. Leave out Blender, Godot and Unreal. *(Nov 17–18)*

3. **Engine health:** no scheduled or background engine checks (they burn usage with no jobs). At job start do a quick file check and reinstall only what was lost after a restart or restore. *(Nov 18)*

4. **Pinned versions:** HyperFrames CLI is pinned to `hyperframes@0.9.52`. *(Nov 18)*

5. **No preset font library;** use engine-native fonts. *(Nov 17)*

## 5. Voice and audio

1. **Best voice for every job.** No fixed shortlist of voices. For every film, search the full ElevenLabs voice library for the voice that best fits the subject, tone and audience, preview the free samples before paying, and log which voice was picked and why. *(Nov 19)* — *Supersedes the earlier preferred-voice pair (Cee Well / Brian).*

2. **ElevenLabs model:** Flash (`eleven_flash_v2_5`); output format `mp3_44100_128` (192 kbps is not allowed on the current tier). *(Nov 17)*

3. **Narration speed 0.85.** *(Nov 17)*

4. **Open item:** final loudness target (−14 vs −15 LUFS) is not settled yet; −14 LUFS is what recent deliveries used. Until Rick decides, film and BYO QA follows `qa.md` (−15 ±1.5 LUFS). Ask Rick before standardising. *(Nov 19)* **Clip `edits.json` exception (Nov 22):** the clip edits format only accepts −14/−16/−18/−23/−24; −15 is silently dropped. Write `loudnessLufs: -14` in clip edits until the app accepts −15. True peak ≤ −1 dBTP remains the house target; meeting it on server-rendered clip output is an app-side issue — log a shortfall, and do not locally re-render without Rick's go.

5. **ElevenLabs music (Nov 19).** We score films with a short cue list (one per mood, at most four) instead of writing a one-off synthetic score. Generate instrumental music for narration with ElevenLabs Music; decide where it enters, turns, stops and resolves. Generate short cues at their true length; for sections over two minutes generate two to three minutes and loop on bar boundaries with overlap crossfades. Join into the existing 48-kHz `audio/music.wav` and duck 10–18 dB using the unchanged mixer. The ending lands on the last frame. Cache paid cues, stay near a $2 music ceiling within the overall budget, and fall back to procedural music if unavailable. Honor customer-supplied music and quiet edits. **Use only under the account's applicable commercial terms.**

6. **Music sits behind the voice (Nov 20).** Under narration, music must feel tucked in the back, never as loud as the words. Duck music at least 18 dB under the voice while anyone speaks (the Keith Murray film at ~11.6 dB with a busy drum break was too loud), and lower it further when the cue has hard drums. Check it: measure music vs voice short-term loudness during speech (music ≥18 LU below) before render; music can come up between lines and on clip-free beats.

7. **Score vs song, chosen per film (Nov 20).** Decide for every cue whether the moment needs music (a groove with drums) or score (no drums: pads, strings, piano, tension, ambience). Serious, sad, dark or scary subjects usually get score. Match genre and era to the subject: old-school boom-bap only for old-school subjects; current subjects get current sounds (modern 808-led rap, drill, R&B, electronic, cinematic, whatever fits). Write that choice into each ElevenLabs prompt. Vary it across films; no default genre. These genres are examples only, not a menu: the type of video and its mood decide the music every time. *(Nov 20)*

8. **Clip audio (Nov 20).** When a clip has someone talking or explaining (interview, news, speech, testimony), play the clip's own audio: pause narration around it and duck the score under it. Clips that are mostly background music stay muted under narration. Music videos are case by case. Keep music ducked under narration as in rule 6.

## 6. Review

1. **Review at 480p.** Full size only for a single frame that can't be judged at 480p. The delivered film is still at the customer's requested resolution. *(Nov 17–18)*

2. **Review with browser stills, not renders.** Open the scene, seek to the exact time, capture a still. For engines that can't run in a browser, use the cheapest way to see the frame; if a render is unavoidable, render only that frame or those few seconds. *(Nov 18)*

3. **What to look at:** each scene's first frame, busiest moment and last frame, plus wherever something changes. A held shot needs one look; where there is motion, check enough frames along the move to confirm positioning and readability. Titles at their longest line, transitions at the overlap, both sides of a touched cut. Lean review is not an excuse to skip needed checks. *(Nov 18)*

4. **Fix rounds:** fix the scene and re-inspect only the frame (or few seconds) that exposed the problem. Targeted fix rounds have no cap; aim to prevent problems up front so first review is mostly taste. *(Nov 18)*

5. **Footage is built into the browser scene** with the graphics, so footage and graphics are seen together during construction and review. *(Nov 18)* — *A render-speed change that would composite footage outside the browser has been proposed but is NOT approved; this rule stands until Rick says otherwise.*

## 12. Variety and mix across films (Rick, Nov 21)

Rick reviewed all 18 films in the style ledger on Nov 21. Palettes and fonts changed every time, but the ideas and the toolkit kept repeating: paper, stamp, typewriter and receipt "document" worlds (6 of 18 films); the same claim device on beef and news stories (red allegation tags, strike-throughs, ALLEGEDLY stamps); a "the whole film is one object" concept every time; a heavy display font plus a monospace label font in nearly every film; dark grounds with one hot accent; and, since Nov 19, almost every film built as one flat Canvas 2D page with atlas frame packs. Real 3D, 2.5D parallax, cut-out photos and camera moves had disappeared. That is what made the earlier films feel good. These rules fix it. Rick's main point is **mix and match**.

1. **Every film gets real depth, but not the whole film.** Every film has at least one real 3D space or 2.5D parallax moment with real camera movement through it: three.js/Theatre scenes, or cut-out photo layers (rembg / SAM 2) separated in depth with a moving camera. Use them where they serve the story. They should not carry the whole film.

2. **Cut-out photos in every film.** At least one beat uses subjects cut out of real photos or frames (rembg / SAM 2), composited in parallax or collage, not just photos inside rectangles.

3. **Mix the edit modes in every film.** Some stretches are footage-led: real clips play full-frame, ungraded or lightly graded, with no designed frame, window or chrome. Other stretches punch into designed graphics. Neither mode covers the whole film. When a person is talking, their real audio plays and the score ducks.

4. **Mixed media and real texture when it fits:** collage, real scanned or photographed textures (fabric, concrete, film, print, paint), colourful or light grounds. These depend on the subject, so use judgment. But dark-ground-plus-one-accent can't be used on more than 2 films in a row.

5. **Mix and match the engines.** Survey the full engine library (`engines.md` plus `engine-library.md`, about 18 engines) on every film. No film is built in a single engine. Each film uses at least 3 engines across its layers, and at least one engine that the previous film didn't use. Rotate through the library over time to see what looks good. Engines tied to a subject (Manim for maths, MapLibre/deck.gl for maps, D3 for data) are used when the subject fits, so look for those chances.

6. **Use the ledger to vary, not just to avoid colours.** Before locking `visualDna`, compare the plan with the last 5 ledger entries (and every entry for the same customer or the same subject) on: concept family, claim device, ground, type formula, transition family, edit-mode mix, 3D/2.5D moments and engines. Within those 5 films, don't repeat a concept family or a claim device. Paper, stamp, typewriter, receipt or document worlds are allowed sometimes, but never twice within 5 films. The display-plus-monospace type formula is used on at most every other film.

7. **Reuse elements only by switching them.** An element from an earlier film may come back if it's changed enough to look different: a different material, motion, scale, engine or role in the story. For example, a stamp becomes a projected light, a 3D press or a sound-led hit instead of a flat ink stamp. Note the switch in the ledger entry.

8. **Same subject, new concept.** A second or later film on the same person or story (for example Ray J) must use a different concept family and claim device from every earlier film on that subject.

9. **Ledger entries record the new fields** (see the format in `STYLE_LEDGER.md`): concept family, claim device, ground, type formula, edit-mode mix (rough % full-frame footage vs designed), 3D/2.5D moments, cut-outs, and engines used.

10. **These are judgment rules, not a gate.** Never fail or stop a job over them. If a rule truly doesn't fit a film, break it on purpose and write why in the job log and the ledger entry.

12. **3D is an accent, not the whole film (Rick, Nov 22).** After the YouTube inauthentic-content film (msaj_5659e4cc), where a 3D conveyor belt carried most of the film, Rick said a fully 3D film looks cheesy. Use real 3D as accents: background depth, 3D text or titles, one or two standout moments. 2.5D parallax (cut-out layers with camera moves) is welcome and can carry more of the film. The rest is designed 2D graphics and real footage. Only build a film that is mostly or entirely 3D when it will look absolutely amazing for that story; otherwise stay away from it. Rule 1's "every film gets real depth" still applies, as a moment, not the backbone.

13. **3D must be super clean and premium (Rick, Nov 22).** Everything in the look is supposed to be super clean. On msaj_5659e4cc, even the 3D itself was cheap: flat lighting, plain cards and a basic belt, built in about 19 minutes. Any 3D or 2.5D moment needs real lighting and materials, soft shadows, depth of field, believable surfaces and textures, and clean edges and anti-aliasing. Check it on frames next to the real footage. If it doesn't hold up next to the footage, rework it, and if it still can't, cut it. Low effort on the 3D counts as unfinished work, under the same 25–30+ minute scene-building rule.
