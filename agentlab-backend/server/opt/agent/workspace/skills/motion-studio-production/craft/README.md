# Craft playbook: how a professional edits (read on every edit or film job)

This is the working craft reference for every Motion Studio job: edits of customer footage (BYOF), clip packs and films made from scratch. It is judgment, not a checklist. Each section gives the principle, how to judge it from frames and audio, what pros do, and the novice trap. Numbers are sourced guidelines, not quotas.

**Precedence.** The live `SKILL.md`, `qa.md` and `delivery.md` win over anything here. Where this playbook and a live rule differ, follow the live rule and note it in `log/job.md`. `craft-standards.md` stays the per-type standards file; this playbook is the editor's way of working on top of it.

## Rick's lines that hold everywhere
- Judgment over hard-coded rules. Decide from the frames what kind of video this is and what a top editor would do with it.
- No templates and no pre-made graphics. Every graphic is designed for this video.
- Graphics never cover the person or what they are showing. They go in empty space, behind the subject, or in a planned full-frame breakout.
- Enough graphics to lift the edit. Too few is a failure, and so is covering the subject.
- Keep a film's signature layout as the base and vary it at key moments. Don't remove it, and don't lock it on screen the whole time.
- Face tracking only for pure talking or podcast footage. Otherwise frame for the content (the board, screen, product or demo).
- BYOF: the customer's direction sets the type of edit. No research. Script and voiceover are optional per job and per segment; default to the speakers' own words. See `byo-editing.md` and `SKILL.md` §4.
- Captions are always a separate file or track, never burned in.
- Voice (films): the best fit from the full ElevenLabs library, Flash model.
- Review stills at 480p; full size only for a frame you can't judge at 480p.

## The order pros work in
1. Look at everything first: scrub all the footage at speed and note faces, energy, visual anchors and what is on the board or screen. The transcript is an index, not the edit.
2. Assembly, then rough cut (does the story work?), then fine cut (rhythm, performance, frame-level timing), then finishing (sound, color, graphics, captions). Don't polish frames before the story works.
3. Check the rough cut (timings, frame strips, audio) before any graphics are built (`review.md` §1).
4. Write the decisions down. Put 5–10 lines in `log/job.md`: the type, what the viewer needs to see per section, why each moment was chosen, the structure you picked over the alternative, the design system and the look statement.

## Quick card: the working practice at each stage
- **Select:** pick moments by performance (eyes, voice, energy), not just the transcript. Compare retakes line by line. Log why each was chosen.
- **Structure:** write two structures, assemble the stronger one, kill darlings, and plant setups for payoffs.
- **Cut:** cut where the thought lands (blink, breath, look), and cut long when in doubt. Use straight picture cuts by default; dissolve only with meaning. Make jump cuts a decision.
- **Hold:** keep pauses that do work, stay on the person the moment is about, and shape tension and release.
- **Meaning:** tighten and reorder, but the speaker must recognize every line as what they meant.
- **Sound:** room tone under every join, tiny fades, J/L cuts, steady level from line to line, music edited to phrase and landing. Loudness is the job's `qa.md` target.
- **Rough-cut check before any graphics:** cut timings against the transcript, frame strips across every cut, and the audio checks. No playback watch; we only see stills.
- **Frame:** frame for the content, and face-track only pure talk or podcasts. Leave room for the coming graphic.
- **Graphics:** a per-film design system drawn from the footage, every graphic with a job and a beat, enough to lift the edit, placed in empty space, behind the subject, or in a planned breakout.
- **Color:** correct and match the customer's footage before any look, protect skin and readable detail, and grade graphics into the look.
- **Captions:** a separate track, with line length and reading speed checked, breaks by sense, and changes snapped to cuts.
- **Notes:** fix the problem behind the note, not the literal note.

## What a cut is for (Murch's Rule of Six)
Emotion 51%, story 23%, rhythm 10%, eye trace 7%, 2D screen grammar 5%, 3D continuity 4%. When two rules fight, the higher one wins, and the frame decides. Source: Murch, *In the Blink of an Eye* ([excerpts](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/6185-in-the-blink-of-an-eye-a-perspective-on-film-editing)).
