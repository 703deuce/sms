# By video type: what each kind of video is for

First name the type in plain words, including mixes ("whiteboard lesson with talking stretches"). Then answer it per section, because the type can change inside one video. These are judgment notes, not modes with locked tools. If footage fits none of them, reason from what the viewer needs to see. Detailed per-type standards are in `../craft-standards.md` Part 2. On BYOF jobs, the customer's direction picks the edit type, with no research and no voiceover.

## Talking head (one person, to camera)
- **The viewer needs:** the person: eyes, expression, usually hands.
- **Frame:** face-led. Face tracking is right here, with modest headroom, a stable frame, eyes near the upper third and nothing cropped. Offset the subject when a graphic needs room.
- **Cut:** performance-led selection (`editing.md` §1). Jump cuts are a dialect only when intended. Otherwise hide them with a clear size change, a meaningful cutaway, or a cut on a gesture. Keep the pauses that land a point.
- **Graphics:** beside or behind the person, in genuinely empty space. Use kinetic emphasis on load-bearing words, numbers that land, and diagrams that rebuild what's explained. Never block the mouth or eyes. Use punch-ins sparingly, for lines that earn them.
- **Trap:** an unbroken face with ums removed and nothing else. Rick: "Don't just take out the ums."

## Podcast / conversation (two or more people)
- **The viewer needs:** who is talking to whom, and the reactions.
- **Frame:** face-led for each speaker, but keep the geography (the 180° line, eyelines). Use splits or stacks for back-and-forth exchanges (`../craft-standards.md` §2.1). Don't track faces in a way that destroys the sense of a shared space.
- **Cut:** cut to the listener as often as the speaker, with L-cuts for reactions. Keep laughs and overlaps when they're the moment.
- **Graphics:** names and key points in unused areas. They shouldn't leap onto a face every time someone talks.
- **Sound:** match levels between the mics, and keep room tone continuous across the speakers' channels.
- **Trap:** a radio show with pictures that only ever cuts to whoever is talking.

## Whiteboard / tutorial / screen share / demo
- **The viewer needs:** the board, the screen, the cursor, the hands at work. These are the subject.
- **Frame:** content-led. Zoom toward the region being written or the control being used, hold through the action, then pull back for context. Show the face for intros and pure-talk stretches only. No face tracking while they draw or demo.
- **Cut:** cut the dead time while something is drawn or loading, but keep enough of the action that the viewer sees how it was done. Speed-ramp long drawing if clarity survives.
- **Graphics:** clean up and rebuild what's drawn (a diagram that redraws the sketch, labels that track the line on the board, a cursor highlight, a step counter) in empty margins or as a planned breakout. Never over the writing, the cursor or the person.
- **Color:** keep the board white without clipping the marker strokes, and the screen readable.
- **Trap:** our whiteboard test: face-tracked, about 20% of the board visible, a few graphics sitting in the middle over him.

## Documentary / YouTube explainer film
- **The viewer needs:** testimony and presence (A-roll) plus evidence and world (B-roll, archive, maps, data).
- **Cut:** build from selects sorted by subject. Assembly → rough → fine. Give sequences a shape (setup, development, turn), not a constant cut rate. Cover interview cuts only with B-roll that means something; an honest jump is better than decoration. Let hard answers breathe.
- **Graphics:** maps, timelines and data that show what footage can't. Visual anchors come first.
- **Sound:** music supports and never drowns the voice. Real clip audio plays where it carries the moment (a live rule: never voiceover the whole time).
- **Color:** match archive and mixed sources into one grade, and protect skin.
- **Ethics:** never move a reaction to a different question (`editing.md` §6).
- **Trap:** literal stock B-roll ("cloud" shown as clouds), and music wallpaper.

## Commercial / product / brand
- **The viewer needs:** the product working, the benefit, and the proof.
- **Cut:** hook, escalation, climax, then a button. Tighter pace, but every hero shot holds long enough to read the product. Build cuts to the music's phrases.
- **Frame:** the product is the subject. Use clean negative space for type, and recompose for each aspect.
- **Graphics:** callouts that track the real object, benefit type with one idea per card, and a title that carries the idea. Brand kit per `../brand-and-type.md`.
- **Color:** product colors stay true, since a brand color shifted by the look is a defect.
- **Trap:** a logo first, generic swooshes, and every shot the same length.

## Social vertical clip (Reels, TikTok, Shorts)
- **The viewer needs:** the point in the first seconds, readable on a phone with the sound off.
- **Edit it as what it is** (talking head, tutorial, doc fragment) inside a 9:16 frame. Vertical doesn't change the type.
- **Hook:** the first frame works as a poster. Put the content proposition within 3 s (TikTok ad guidance), and pay it off. Payoffs still need a beat.
- **Placement:** design inside the UI safe areas (`../qa.md` §2 item 9), then look at the real frame. The safe middle band is often the person's body, so use empty space, behind the subject, or a breakout.
- **Type:** large, short, high-contrast, held still long enough to read. Captions stay a separate track; there are no word-pop burned-in captions.
- **Trap:** the AI-short look: word-pop captions, emojis, a punch zoom on every line, and the same template as every third Reel.

## Music-driven (music video, montage, hype edit, sports highlight)
- **The viewer needs:** energy shaped by the track, and the key moments.
- **Cut:** edit the music first to phrases and a real ending, then lock the big hits to downbeats and let the rest breathe around them. Don't sync everything ("robotic", Machliss).
- **Dynamics:** quiet sections and drops, the way music has them, with shots shortening into peaks.
- **Montage:** give it its own internal arc that escalates. A montage is not a slideshow.
- **Sound:** keep natural sound (crowd, impact) under or between the music where it adds reality (`../craft-standards.md` §2.3 for sports).
- **Trap:** every cut on every beat, so the edit feels mechanical.

## Observational / vérité / vlog (little dialogue)
- Slow down, follow action and looks, and let moments play. Don't force talking-head grammar. Use sound and ambience to carry continuity.

## Mixed videos
Most real footage mixes types. Decide per section, and change the framing, density and pace when the content changes. The signature layout is the constant underneath (`design-and-motion.md` §1).
